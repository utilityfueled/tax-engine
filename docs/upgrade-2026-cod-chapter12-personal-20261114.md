# Chapter 12 personal debt and original bankruptcy case history

The continuing-individual cancellation method now admits a complete original Chapter 12 discharge of unsecured personal borrowing. Both Chapter 12 and the existing Chapter 13 route require an affirmative original-source answer that the case was never under Chapter 7 or Chapter 11. An omitted answer remains unknown and prevents calculation; an explicit negative answer also prevents calculation. Existing saved Chapter 13 facts acquire no inferred history or automatic confirmation.

The scope is personal living-expense borrowing with the existing exact discharge, nondeductible personal-interest, owner, court, complete asset/liability and independent regular/AMT attribute records. Farm, business, investment and mixed borrowing, deductible interest, property transfers, joint/community allocations, basis-first elections and a separate bankruptcy estate remain outside this method. Selecting Chapter 12 does not admit those other facts.

## Controlling sources retrieved November 14, 2026

[IRC §1398(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t26/s1398/) applies the separate taxable estate method to an individual case under Chapter 7 or 11. [IRC §1399](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t26/s1399/) supplies the corresponding no-separate-entity rule outside §1398. [Publication 908 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p908), “Individuals in Chapter 12 or 13,” expressly places the annual income and debt-cancellation attribute reductions on the individual's return. Its conversion discussion expressly describes Chapter 11 to Chapter 13 termination of the separate estate. The broader need to establish ownership when a case was previously under Chapter 7 or 11 follows from the separate-estate and succession rules in §1398, together with §1399; that broader application is an inference from those statutes, not a quoted Chapter 12 conversion example in Publication 908. A discharge chapter alone consequently does not prove the complete case history or attribute ownership.

[IRC §108(a)(1)(A), (a)(2)(A), (b) and (d)(2)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t26/s108/) provide the title 11 exclusion, its priority over insolvency, after-tax attribute reductions and the court-jurisdiction/discharge definition. [IRC §1017(b)(2), (c)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t26/s1017/) retains the basis cap and exempt-property protection. The matching IRS explanations are in [Publication 4681 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p4681) and the [Form 982 instructions (12/2021)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i982). No new controlling interpretation is needed to distinguish original Chapters 12/13 from the separate-estate cases. Qualified human review of the implementation and expected results remains outstanding.

## Remaining Chapter 7/11 engineering contract

Chapters 7 and 11 are not blocked merely by a specialist label. They require a separate debtor/estate ownership and timing implementation that this continuing-individual method cannot supply. A viable next source contract must bind commencement and conversion/termination/dismissal dates, original chapter history, debtor identity and estate EIN, short-year election and tax-year boundaries, estate/debtor income ownership, and the original attribute succession under §1398(g)/(i). The estate's own Form 1041 and after-tax Form 982 reductions must not silently become the individual's current-year attributes. Conversion/dismissal cases also need the actual returned attribute and basis records. No such dates, elections, separate-estate returns or attribute balances are invented by the new history confirmation.
